# Mastercard Cybersecurity Virtual Experience

## Project Overview

This project was completed as part of the Mastercard Cybersecurity Virtual Experience on Forage.

The simulation focused on phishing awareness, identifying employee vulnerabilities and developing security awareness training to reduce the risk of phishing attacks.

## Task 1: Phishing Email Simulation

- Reviewed examples of phishing emails and identified common warning signs.
- Explored how attackers use impersonation, urgency and suspicious links to deceive employees.
- Developed a simulated phishing email for an authorised security awareness exercise.
- Considered the importance of appropriate sender details, approved training links and email presentation.

## Task 2: Phishing Awareness and Training

Analysed the results of a simulated phishing campaign across seven departments.

### Campaign Findings

| Department | Click-through rate | Phishing success rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| HR | 85% | 75% |
| Marketing | 40% | 38% |
| Card Services | 50% | 10% |
| Reception | 10% | 0% |
| Engineering | 4% | 1% |
| R&D | 5% | 2% |
| IT | 2% | 0% |

HR and Marketing were identified as the priority departments for additional awareness training because they recorded the highest phishing success rates.

### Training Recommendations

Created a four-slide phishing awareness presentation for HR and Marketing covering:

1. Introduction to phishing and its risks.
2. Common phishing tactics, including impersonation, urgency and malicious attachments.
3. Practical phishing examples relevant to HR and Marketing.
4. Steps employees should take to identify, verify and report suspicious messages.

## Skills Practised

- Phishing awareness and analysis
- Social engineering awareness
- Interpreting security campaign results
- Identifying departmental risk areas
- Security awareness training design
- Communicating security recommendations

## Key Takeaway

The exercise demonstrated how phishing campaign results can help organisations identify areas that need additional security awareness training. It also reinforced the importance of educating employees to recognise, verify and report suspicious communications.
